For many professionals, the gap isn't grammar. It's exposure — you simply haven't met enough of the language people actually use at work.

Why it happens

Business English is more than grammar

Workplace conversation runs on a repeating set of expressions. They aren't difficult words — they're familiar words arranged in ways you have to have met before to use comfortably.

Knowing an expression academically is not the same as recognising it instantly in a call and reaching for it without thinking. That comes from seeing it in context, with the meaning, the tone and a real example beside it.

"Let's circle back."

"Can you run that by me?"

"What's the bottom line?"

"Let's put a pin in it."

"We need everyone on board."

How to use it

Don't memorise 300 expressions at once

The book is written as a reference you return to — not a course you finish. Here's the approach it recommends.

STEP 1

Pick the chapter closest to your actual work this month.

STEP 2

Take 5–10 expressions — no more than that in a week.

STEP 3

Read each workplace example out loud so the rhythm sticks.

STEP 4

Start noticing them in meetings, emails, calls and videos.

STEP 5

Use one or two where they genuinely fit — then repeat.
